### Executive recommendation

State whether evidence supports a paper, a narrower paper or a stop/defer decision. Name the central contribution or explain why it cannot yet be defended. Summarize decisive evidence, largest uncertainty, most valuable next action and required supervisor decision.

### One-sentence contribution

State one bounded contribution, not a topic, application goal, observation list or untested mechanism.

### Existing evidence versus required work

Separate source-verified observations, derived/unchecked evidence, hypotheses and evidence still to be generated.

## 1. Research diagnosis and evidence readiness

Explain the scientific problem, project system and important findings as a connected account. Identify robust observations, measurement/comparison uncertainties and claims that cannot yet be made.

Provide an evidence map: central claim/question; supporting source IDs; contradictory/contextual/audit evidence; confidence/limitations; missing decisive evidence and its task. Explain alternatives and conditions for narrowing/stopping. Make measurement definitions, provenance, uncertainty and comparability explicit.

## 2. Literature frontier and novelty boundary

Record discovery routes, dates, representative query families, coverage of synonyms/formulas/mechanisms/adjacent systems and citation trails, plus search/access failures. Compare closest primary studies by actual system, transformation direction, medium/conditions, mechanism, impurity/structure role, measurands, demonstrated result and limitation. Separate source statements from interpretation. Include contradictory work and publication-status checks. Explain what is known, unresolved and genuinely different. Bound every gap statement by inspected evidence; a null search result is not a novelty claim. Do not claim priority beyond the search.

## 3. Scientific-mainline decision, central question and contribution

First state the user's original intended scientific question/hypothesis (or that none was supplied). Compare it with evidence-led candidates, any independently defensible method/measurement candidate, conditional/hybrid options and stop/defer. For each serious candidate state nearest prior art and exact distinction, strongest supporting and contradicting evidence, alternative explanations, decisive missing evidence, feasible recovery path, readiness and disposition. Explain why the selected mainline is preferable. Do not pivot automatically to an audit/validation paper merely because the current dataset is incomplete; do not preserve an unsupported mechanism merely because it was the original intent.

Then state the selected central question, thesis and one-sentence contribution with boundaries. Explain strongest evidence, weakest link, alternatives, assumptions and decisive falsification tests. Distinguish the central contribution from supporting findings, validation tasks and side stories.

## 4. Introduction architecture and draft

Provide paragraph sequence without a fixed count. For every paragraph specify role/message, verified literature support, connection from the prior paragraph, transition to the next, unresolved citations/claims and readiness. Add source-grounded English draft prose where justified; unsupported premises remain placeholders or are omitted.

## 5. Complete manuscript storyline

Explain the whole argument from opening problem to final conclusion. For each Results/Discussion section or major paragraph cluster specify question, evidence and status, key comparison/controls, responsible observation, inference/limitations, alternative explanation, role in the contribution and transition. Avoid instrument-by-instrument chaptering unless the science requires it.

Provide working title, abstract argument map and conclusion architecture. Expected outcomes must not be written as facts.

## 6. TOC and main Figure/Panel architecture

### TOC/graphical abstract brief

State whether required/useful under current journal instructions. Describe visual message/elements/intended inference; mark concept versus completed artwork.

### Figure sequence and Panel specifications

For every Figure give message, argument role, manuscript destination and composition. For every Panel specify question; exact content; source files/Regions/Result Units/Data Slots and status; existing/reprocessed/new/blocked state; groups/conditions/controls/comparison; analysis/statistics/axes/units/annotations where known; intended/prohibited inferences; linked tasks/SI; and scientific acceptance criterion.

Do not force a fixed Figure count. Missing-data Panels must be marked planned/blocked and must not look like observed results.

## 7. Supporting Information architecture

For every SI Text/Table/Figure specify ID/title, exact content, source/task, status, why it belongs in SI, main claims/sections/Figures/Panels supported, QC/reproducibility checks and remaining uncertainty. Do not force an SI count or hide decisive evidence in SI.

## 8. Experiment and data-analysis work packages

For each task state: objective/question; why necessary and which uncertainty/claim it addresses; exact inputs and missing requirements; variables/groups/conditions/sampling unit/comparison design; method/design/controls and justified replicate/statistical considerations; raw/processed outputs; provenance/QC/uncertainty/completion criteria; supportive/contradictory/mixed/inconclusive interpretations; dependencies/blocker/priority/role; destination in claim/section/Figure/Panel/SI.

Unknown values remain TBD. Do not invent local protocols, equipment, amounts, parameters or replicates.

## 9. Theory and computational collaboration briefs

Include only methods with a defined decision value. Specify scientific question/inference; model construction and source structure/composition; inputs/prerequisites; relevant states/charges/boundaries/conditions; method rationale; known settings versus TBD; convergence/sensitivity; observables/output files; controls/alternate models; validation against experiments; limits; linked claims/Figures/SI and downstream decision.

A method name alone is not a handoff. Block work that lacks essential inputs and name those inputs.

## 10. Prioritized execution and dependencies

Give justified priority order across verification/reanalysis, experiments, computation, Figure/SI preparation and writing. State deliverable, priority reason, prerequisite, decision unlocked and completion/stop condition. Identify parallel versus dependent tasks and stop/re-scope gates.

## 11. Risks, limitations and alternative outcomes

Discuss scientific, measurement, literature, feasibility and interpretation risks; their effect on the contribution; how to detect/manage them; and observations that distinguish alternative explanations.

## 12. Supervisor decisions and team assignment

List approval decisions with evidence and trade-offs. Translate approved priorities into assignable work packages with exact inputs and return files. Keep unapproved decisions pending.

## 13. Evidence and references

List actual source records and literature used, with IDs, verified URLs/DOIs, access tier, source location and limitations. Separate full-text-verified claims from abstract/metadata-only context.

## Final consistency statement

Explain how the central contribution is carried through Introduction, storyline, Figures, SI, work packages and priorities. Name any remaining break. Validator PASS is not proof of scientific validity.
