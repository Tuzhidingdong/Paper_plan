# Literature evidence and novelty discipline

## Evidence hierarchy
1. Publisher-hosted or repository full text actually inspected.
2. Abstract actually inspected.
3. Bibliographic metadata only.
4. Search snippets or third-party summaries are discovery leads, not verified primary evidence.

Assign every paper one access tier. Record the source URL, DOI, access date, exact scope inspected and short exact snippets with section/page/figure/table locations. Each evidence snippet receives a Python-issued SN ID. Never elevate access level because a paper is cited by another source.

## Claim support
A literature claim is a bounded statement about what a cited source actually reports. One literature claim may be supported by multiple snippets; conflicting reports must remain visible. Do not stretch abstract wording into details about synthesis conditions, uncertainty or mechanism. Metadata-only records may remain in the competitor bibliography but cannot substantiate scientific statements.

## Gap tests
A defensible gap should identify:
- target material/system and process boundary;
- specific scientific uncertainty or unresolved comparison;
- what the best competing studies already show;
- conditions under which those studies are comparable;
- exact missing discriminating evidence;
- search date, source systems, date range and keyword families;
- strongest counter-example and residual uncertainty.

Do not use “first”, “unprecedented” or “never reported” unless a sufficiently bounded search and primary-source comparison supports that exact scope. Prefer “the searched literature indicates…” plus limitations.

## Benchmark matrix
Compare only verified like-for-like conditions. Track sample composition and preparation, loading/mass basis, reaction medium, pH, temperature, time, concentration, normalization, analytical method, conversion/selectivity/rate definition, controls and replicate/statistical reporting. Use “not reported in inspected source” rather than filling blanks.
