# Manuscript story design reference

A manuscript is a connected scientific argument, not an ordered inventory of techniques.

## Core structure
- Problem: what important scientific or practical question motivates the work?
- State of knowledge: what have the strongest relevant studies established?
- Limitation: what exactly remains unresolved, and why does it matter?
- Question: what does this study test?
- Evidence sequence: what is the shortest sufficient chain of results?
- Interpretation: what explanations are supported, weakened or unresolved?
- Contribution: what new knowledge can be claimed within the evidence boundary?
- Implication and limitation: what follows, and what does not?

## One central contribution
One central contribution should organize the paper. Related secondary findings may strengthen it. Unrelated observations should be deferred or assigned to a separate project rather than joined by a superficial theme.

## Introduction
The paragraph count is not fixed. Each paragraph has one rhetorical job, verified literature support and a clear transition. Use the actual paper logic and journal conventions rather than a universal template.

## Falsifiability
For every proposed causal or mechanistic explanation, specify what observation would weaken or reject it and what competing explanation remains plausible.
